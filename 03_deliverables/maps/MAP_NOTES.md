# Corridor boundary map · method and sources
*Updated 2026-07-04. Supersedes the original five-road boundary from the spin-out.*

## Map inclusion rule (the compute-nexus test; locked by Paul 2026-07-05)

A site earns a dot only if its headline work is (a) light or photonics itself, or (b) generates, stores, moves, or cools the electricity that powers the computing corridor. Clean-energy work whose output is transport fuel or vehicles (biofuels, EV drivetrains) does not qualify, even though it is "energy," because it does not power or cool compute. Cooling counts (data centers need it). Wind and water are noted in popups where they co-locate but do not earn their own dots. No private companies. No dot without a thesis tie. This rule reproduces every case decision to date: solar, fusion, grid/ARIES, hydrogen storage, thermal/cooling, photonics, and quantum optics pass; NLR's biofuels (IBRF, FTLB) and vehicle electrification (VTIF) fail.

## Revision 7x (2026-09-22): the atlas published to Felt; no roster or convention change

The atlas was published as a live Felt map: https://felt.com/map/Photon-Mountain-the-corridor-built-with-light-OSOsyDNWTDe1acx9BDkkJ4D, set to view-only by link. **Nothing in this folder changed.** The roster is the same 53 markers in the same seven categories at the same coordinates; no locked convention was touched. This entry records the publication and the two decisions it forced.

Layer split. One layer per locked category, seven in all, over the boundary polygon on a dark basemap, so a reader can toggle a category off. Each dot carries its GeoJSON `note` in the popup, which keeps the per-marker honesty framing (approximate pins, campus pins, horizon items) attached to the dot rather than stranded in this file. Boundary simplification, and why. Felt's presigned upload endpoint sits outside the sandbox's network allowlist, so the file-upload path was unavailable and the layers went in as inline GeoJSON instead. The boundary ring is 1,679 vertices per `photon_mountain_corridor.geojson`, which put it over a practical size limit for an inline paste. The published ring is therefore simplified to 207 vertices at an 8 m Douglas-Peucker tolerance, the same tolerance `photon_mountain_boundary_map.html` already uses for its own display ring. **The full trace stays canonical in `photon_mountain_corridor.geojson` and `photon_mountain_corridor.kml`.** Treat the Felt boundary as a display ring, not as the measurement, exactly as the HTML map's ring is treated. Anyone re-publishing should say so.

Same day, the atlas also became the source for a generated vision infographic at `../infographics/photon_mountain_vision_build.py`, which reads this folder's GeoJSON directly. A roster change now regenerates that page, so `04_tools/marker_parity.py` guards one more consumer than it did.

## The six roads

The loop uses the minimum set of roads we could find that closes around all six places:

1. **CO-93, continuing as Broadway** (west edge): Golden to north Boulder. CO-93 officially ends at US-36 in south Boulder; Broadway carries the same line north through downtown.
2. **US-36** (northeast edge): from the Broadway junction in north Boulder, down 28th Street, then the Denver-Boulder Turnpike to I-25.
3. **I-25** (link): US-36 merge south to the I-70 interchange.
4. **I-70** (north Denver edge): I-25 east to Colorado Blvd (exit 276).
5. **Colorado Blvd** (east edge): I-70 south to 6th Ave. This stretch is Colorado State Highway 2.
6. **6th Avenue / US-6** (south edge): Colorado Blvd west through Denver and Lakewood to Golden, ending at CO-93. East of I-25 this is Denver's 6th Avenue; the US-6 designation leaves 6th Avenue at I-25.

Why not fewer: Union Station forces the east closure to run east of downtown (I-25 alone passes west of it), which costs the I-70 + Colorado Blvd pair; Boulder needs both Broadway and 28th Street to box the downtown station and campus; the south and west edges are single roads already.

## Tracing method

Road centerlines were traced from OpenStreetMap via the OSRM public router (router.project-osrm.org), then spliced and verified programmatically (point-in-polygon plus distance-to-boundary for all six places, tested against 12 probe points). Two short straight stitches, each under 150 m, close gaps the router would not drive: at the US-36/Broadway junction in north Boulder and on 6th Avenue at Speer Blvd (one-way carriageway break). The HTML map uses a simplified ring (8 m tolerance); the GeoJSON/KML carry the full trace.

Correction (same day): the first published trace contained router artifacts that put fragments of streets outside the six roads into the line: a side-street tab at the Broadway splice in north Boulder, a jog off 6th Avenue onto the parallel one-way block near I-25, and a surface-street detour (58th Ave, Logan, 56th, Lincoln, Broadway) along the I-25 leg caused by a routing waypoint that snapped to a frontage street. All were removed by re-tracing those stretches against the eastbound 6th Avenue carriageway and verified I-25 mainline points; the ring was also checked for self-intersections (none remain).
