## The position, in one line

**The corridor's manufacturing play is photonic and mature-node specialty fabrication, built on a research base no leading-edge state can claim.**

The corridor will not out-fab Taiwan or Ohio on leading-edge logic, and it does not need to. Its fit is the light layer: photonic chips on mature nodes, made next to the science that invents them.

## The honest siting picture

Leading-edge logic fabs are the most resource-intensive thing in the industry. EUV lithography is enormously power-hungry, the precision demands the most ultrapure water, and the process runs hundreds of exacting steps. Those fabs go where the water and stable geology already are. The big announced buildouts chose the Great Lakes basin and the Pacific Northwest. Intel delayed its central Ohio fab to a 2030 completion, per CNBC (2026-10-21). Micron's upstate New York megafab makes memory, not logic, and expects first wafers in 2030, per Micron's fiscal 2026 10-K. The Southwest made it work through aggressive incentives and hard water recycling.

Colorado is not in the water-abundant tier for leading-edge logic. Competing head-on for a leading-edge fab would mean bidding against Ohio and New York on water and seismic stability, a losing hand. The corridor does not play it.

## The corridor-credible play

Photonic chips run on mature CMOS nodes. GlobalFoundries' Fotonix platform uses the same 45-nanometer CMOS process as the foundry's digital circuits, per TechInsights. That is a deliberate engineering choice: photonics piggybacks on the well-understood older CMOS ecosystem, not the bleeding edge. A mature-node fab needs meaningfully less water and power than a leading-edge one. For a dry, high state, that turns the water constraint into a siting argument instead of a disqualifier.

The pieces are in place or dated:

- **The research base, operating.** The NIST Boulder Microfabrication Facility is an 18,000-square-foot, ISO class 5 cleanroom, per NIST. NIST teams process integrated photonics there today at the 100 mm wafer scale, per NIST. NIST Boulder and CU Boulder run foundational photonic-chip work: on-chip lasers, packaging for extreme environments, heterogeneous integration of new materials.
- **The fab, targeted for 2026.** The Quantum Commons campus plan in Arvada includes a photonic-integrated-circuit fab. The campus is developed with the Elevate Quantum Tech Hub on a Colorado School of Mines site. Mines set a 2026 target for a 10,000-square-foot fabrication lab and cleanroom building, per its 2024 groundbreaking release. Elevate Quantum's 14,000-square-foot Quantum Commercialization Lab opened on the campus in March 2026, per Elevate Quantum. No fab opening had been announced as of 2026-11-12. Say planned, not operating.
- **The funding, attributed.** Elevate Quantum received a $40.5 million EDA Tech Hubs award in July 2024, per Elevate Quantum and the Colorado governor's office. It was about $41 million, per the EDA's own release. Matching funds from Colorado ($77 million) and New Mexico ($10 million) bring the total to about $127.5 million, per Elevate Quantum.
- **The statewide base, honestly located.** Entegris opened a 135,000-square-foot Manufacturing Center of Excellence in Colorado Springs on 2025-11-05, per the company. Entegris planned about $600 million for the site over several years, per its project infographic. Initial commercial operations began in 2025, per the company. Broadcom runs a major fab in Fort Collins. InnovaFlex Foundry, in Colorado Springs, is the only flat-panel semiconductor foundry outside Asia, per Colorado OEDIT. All three sit outside the corridor; they are the state's supply-chain context, not corridor addresses.
- **The market, growing.** Analysts estimate silicon photonics at roughly $3 billion in 2025, with 2034 estimates running $17 to $29 billion (sourced in `photonics_why_and_how.md`, searched 2026-07-06).

The play also pulls along chip-adjacent manufacturing where NIST-adjacent firms are already strong: photonic components, advanced materials, packaging, and precision test-and-measurement equipment.

## Where quantum fits

The Arvada fab serves photonic quantum as well as classical photonics, and that light-based slice is Photon Mountain's. The broader quantum-manufacturing strategy, and the statewide quantum economy story, belong to the Colorado AI project. This document claims the fab and the photonics; it does not claim the quantum industry.

## What the corridor tells a builder, honestly

Serious, long-horizon manufacturers want predictable permitting, honest infrastructure planning, a real workforce pipeline, and stable rules. The corridor's version of that honesty starts with resources: Colorado's water is a hard physical limit, and the grid is growing but contested. A region honest about that is more credible to a builder, not less, because nobody sites a fab on a promise. The corridor's pitch is calibrated to mature-node reality: the water and power a photonic fab actually needs, which the region can actually supply.

## Two honest caveats

1. **"Lighter" is not "light."** Photonic and mature-node fabrication is still real semiconductor manufacturing: cleanrooms, ultrapure water, substantial power, hundreds of process steps. It sits well below a leading-edge logic fab and well above a precision-instrumentation shop. Calibrate every resource claim to mature-node reality, not to "light manufacturing."
2. **"Light CPUs" is hype-adjacent.** The credible near-term photonics is optical interconnects and inference accelerators, almost all hybrid electronic-photonic designs. A general-purpose light CPU that replaces conventional processors is still mostly research. Speak to accelerators and interconnects, not general-purpose optical processors.

## Internal honesty guardrails

1. **No leading-edge claim, ever.** The corridor's case is specialty and mature-node manufacturing. Any copy implying a leading-edge logic fab belongs here fails the fact-check and the strategy at once.
2. **The fab is planned, not operating.** Colorado School of Mines set a 2026 target for the fab building (2024). The Denver Gazette reported the campus's first phase due by the end of 2026, without saying it includes the fab (2025-08-29). No fab opening was announced as of 2026-11-12. The operating cleanroom is NIST's, in Boulder.
3. **Funding figures stay attributed and current.** The EDA award and state matches are dated 2024 figures from named sources. The seed cited "up to $1 billion over a decade" for the Tech Hubs program; not re-verified this session, not used.
4. **Statewide is not corridor.** Entegris (Colorado Springs), Broadcom (Fort Collins), and InnovaFlex (Colorado Springs) are state context. Name their cities when citing them.
5. **Quantum stays in its light-based lane.** The fab and quantum optics count here. The broad quantum story is Colorado AI's.
